3D Correlation Space

Goal

  • Ultimate goal: Develop an approach that allows us to claim “With 95% confidence, this confidence region contains the true set”.

  • Temporary goal: In reality, the true set is unobserved; we only observe data. Since many causal discovery algorithms operates on the data correlation matrix \(R_{obs}\), our intermediate goal is to generate perturbations around \(R_{obs}\), aiming to represent the sampling distribution of the correlation estimator under repeated sampling. We then use this approximation to construct a confidence region for the underlying population correlation structure, and study how causal discovery perform across this region.

  • Parking-lot questions:

    1. What is our scope, what assumptions are we making:

      We assume infinite sample at this stage, distribution of pearson correlation estimators is asymptotically normal. Want a frequentist approach.

    2. Inference on \(\rho\) :

      Do we think joint-inference, fisher-z transformation approach would work better?

Notations

\(R\): True R, unknown

\(R_{obs}\): Observed R, typically estimate from sample data

\(R^{\prime}_{obs}\): Use superscript “\(\prime\)” to denote a perturbed object

\(\rho\): \(vech(R)\), the unique off-diagonal elements of \(R\) matrix.

\(\hat\rho\): \(vech(R_{obs})\), the unique off-diagonal elements of \(R_{obs}\)

\(\hat\rho^{\prime}\): \(vech(R^{\prime}_{obs})\), the unique off-diagonal elements of \(R^{\prime}_{obs}\)

\(\mu\): population mean

\(\Sigma\): Covariance matrix

\(\Psi\): Covariance matrix of pearson correlation coefficient

\(d\): number of variables in data

\(p\): number of parameters (length of \(\rho\)) = \(\frac{d(d-1)}{2}\)

Asymptotic Distribution

Under standard regularity conditions: normality and finite asypmtotic covariance \(\Psi \in R^{p \times p}\) (and also finite fourth-order moment), CLT shows that \[ \sqrt{n}(\hat{\rho}-\rho) \xrightarrow{d} N(0,\Psi) \]where:

  • diagonal of \(\Psi\): \(\psi_{jk} = 1-\rho_{jk}^2\)

  • off-diagonal of \(\Psi\):

\[ \psi_{jk,hm} \;=\; N\,\sigma_{jk,hm} \;=\; \frac{1}{2}\Big[ (\rho_{jh}-\rho_{jk}\rho_{kh})(\rho_{km}-\rho_{kh}\rho_{hm}) \;+\; (\rho_{jm}-\rho_{jk}\rho_{km})(\rho_{kh}-\rho_{kj}\rho_{jh}) \;+\; \\ (\rho_{jh}-\rho_{jm}\rho_{mh})(\rho_{km}-\rho_{kj}\rho_{jm}) \;+\; (\rho_{jm}-\rho_{jk}\rho_{km})(\rho_{kh}-\rho_{km}\rho_{mh}) \Big]. \]

When \(\rho\) is unknown, we use plug-in estimate \(\hat\rho\).

Can we prove that \(\hat\Psi \xrightarrow{p} \Psi\) ?

Confidence region of \(\rho\)

Let \(Z = \Psi^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\sqrt{n}}(\hat{\rho} - \rho)\), we have \(Z \xrightarrow{d} N(0,I_p)\). Therefore, by continuous mapping theorem, \(Z^TZ \xrightarrow{d} \chi^2_{p}\).

Formally, consider the testing framework:

\(H_0: \rho = \rho_0\) vs \(H_1: \rho \neq \rho_0\)

Under \(H_0\),

\[ \sqrt{n}(\hat{\rho}-\rho_0) \xrightarrow{d} N(0,\Psi) \]

To test the hypothesis jointly for a \(p\)-length vector, consider the test statistic

\[ T = n(\hat{\rho} - \rho_0)^T\Psi^{-1}(\hat{\rho} - \rho_0)\ \leq \chi^{2}_{p, 1-\alpha} \]

We previously show that \(T \xrightarrow{d} \chi^2_{p}\) under \(H_0\). Therefore, when \(T \geq \chi^2_{p, 1-\alpha}\), we reject \(H_0\).

The confidence region can be obtained through test inversion: \(C_{1-\alpha} = \{ \rho: \ n(\hat{\rho} - \rho)^T\Psi^{-1}(\hat{\rho} - \rho)\ \leq \chi^{2}_{p, 1-\alpha} \}\).

All perturbed vectors \(\hat\rho^{\prime}\) satisfying the inequality are accepted as points inside the ellipsoid.

When \(\Psi\) is unknown, by slutsky theorem, \(C_{1-\alpha} = \{ \rho: \ n(\hat{\rho} - \rho)^T\hat\Psi(\hat\rho)^{-1}(\hat{\rho} - \rho)\ \leq \chi^{2}_{p, 1-\alpha} \}\)

  1. I do think the confidence ellipsoid should be the intersect of \(C_{1-\alpha}\) and the PSD criteria.
  2. The plug-in approach is only true when \(\hat\Psi \xrightarrow{p} \Psi\) ? , still need to prove this.

Generating random samples around \(\rho_0\)

What are the different approach to generate random samples?

  1. Generate MVN data based on \(\hat\rho\) and compute its correlation vector \(\hat\rho^{\prime}\)
  2. Generate \(\hat\rho^{\prime}\) from \(\hat\rho\) directly: sample randomly from the full distributional spread around \(\rho_0\) (wald-cloud).

Sample from full distribution of \(\rho_0\)

Let \(\rho_0 = vech(R_0)\). For testing \(H_0: \rho = \rho_0\), based on asymptotic normality of \(\hat\rho\), we have wald-type statistic (same as previously mentioned)

\[ T = n (\hat\rho - \rho_0)^T \Psi(\rho_0)^{-1} (\hat\rho - \rho_0) \sim \chi^2_p \]

This implies we can draw \(\hat\rho\) under the null distribution \(H_0\):

\[ \hat\rho^{(b)} \sim N_p(\rho_0, \frac{\Psi(\rho_0)}{n}) \]

Equivalently, the linear algebra approach:

\(\hat\rho^{(b)} = \rho_0 + Lz\), where \(z \sim N(0,I_p)\), \(L= \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}UD^\frac{1}{2}\) (eigen-decomposition of \(\Psi(\rho_0)\): \(U\) is the eigenvectors , \(D\) is the eigenvalue matrix)

This should give us below statement when \(n\) is infinite, \[ Pr_{\rho_0} (\rho \in C_{1-\alpha}(\hat\rho)) = 1-\alpha \]

Sample inside confidence region of \(\rho_0\)

The goal is to generate uniform and random samples within \(C_{1-\alpha}(\rho_0)\) with guarantee: \(C_{1-\alpha} = \{ \rho: \ n(\hat{\rho} - \rho_0)^T\Psi^{-1}(\hat{\rho} - \rho_0)\ \leq \chi^{2}_{p, 1-\alpha} \}\)

Modifying from the wald-cloud approach (matrix notation), a random uniform sample can be constructed by:

\[ \hat\rho^{(b)} = \rho_0 + L(\sqrt{c} r^{\frac{1}{p}}Z) \]

where \(c: \chi^2_{p, 1-\alpha}\), \(r\) represent a random radius sample from \(Unif(0,1)\), \(Z\) represents the random unit direction, sample from \(N(0,I_p)\) and rescale to have length (norm) 1 (i.e. \(||Z|| = 1\))

My intuition on this:

\(c\) sets the \(1-\alpha\) chi-square cut-off that determines the confidence ellipsoid boundary; \(r\) distributes a random radius (between (0,1)) uniformly in all \(p\)-dimension within the ellipsoid; \(Z\) is the standard way to generate uniform random directions on a unit sphere. Geometrically, multiplying these three objects to \(L\)is like rotating and scaling the ellipsoid.

Why don’t we use the first approach (Generate data then compute correlation) ?

  1. Making extra assumption: This approach assumes the underlying data distribution, which seems to be unnecessary here in our theory. We only need \(\hat\rho\) to be normal (asymptotically).
  2. To meet out goal as a less expensive approach for resampling. Sample from \(\rho\) directly will be a less expensive approach.

Notes:

  1. Previously discuss a sphere-cloud approach, I modified it to the “wald-cloud” (approach 2) above, this is to consider the covariance of correlation under null.

Simulation 1: Nominal vs Actual Coverage

Goal is to validate:

\[ Pr_\rho(\rho \in C_{1-\alpha}(\hat\rho)) = 1-\alpha \]

Frequentist interpretation of confidence region: Over repeated samples, the confidence region (which is random, depends on sample) contains the fixed true \(\rho\) with probability \(1-\alpha\).

Simulation Flow: Monte Carlo estimate of coverage of \(CR_{1-\alpha}(\hat{\rho})\)

Input: \(\rho_0\), \(\Psi(\rho_0)\), sample size \(n\), significance level \(\alpha\), number of replicates \(B\)

Output: Coverage level \(CR_{1-\alpha}(\hat\rho)\)

\(H \leftarrow 0\)

for \(b\) \(\leftarrow\) 1 to \(B\):

  1. Sample \(\hat\rho^{(b)}\) from Wald-cloud (or equivalently, normal distribution)

  2. Compute covariance \(\hat\Psi(\hat\rho^{(b)})\)

  3. Compute mahalanobis distance \(T_b \leftarrow n(\hat{\rho}^{(b)}-\rho_{0})^{\top}[\hat\Psi(\rho_{0})]^{-1}(\hat{\rho}^{(b)}-\rho_{0})\)

  4. if \(T_b \leq \chi^2_{p,1-\alpha}\), then \(H \leftarrow H+1\) ;

return \(H /B\) (actual coverage)

This simulation repeats over different \(\rho_0\)’s, which are uniformly sampled from the rlkj distribution by under different \(\eta\). sample size \(n\) is set to \(1e6\) to mirror aysmptotic-ness, \(\alpha = 0.05, 0.10\), \(B= 200\).
